# Shared - notes for agents

Why the localization layer is the way it is. Translator context lives in `Localizable.xcstrings` and in the `comment:` arguments of `String(localized:)` — those are code, not comments, and stay in the Swift. Read the section of a file before editing it; an edit that changes or cancels a point below updates the point in the same commit.

## BidiText.swift

- `BidiText` — one definition of the wrapper put around a formatted value before it is substituted into a translated sentence, next to `Locale.latinDigits` and for the same reason: a formatting rule that exists at only one call site is a rule the next call site will not know about. Every substitution site (history rows, the last-meal preview, the "fast counts from" subline) reaches for it.
- `BidiText.isolate(_:)` — wraps the value in a Unicode isolate so the sentence around it cannot reorder it and it cannot reorder the sentence. FIRST STRONG ISOLATE (U+2068), not LEFT-TO-RIGHT ISOLATE: the direction is read off the value's own first strong character rather than asserted, and both readings of a clock time need that. "19:00" has no strong character — a bare digit run whose place would otherwise be settled by whatever sits beside it. "8:00 م" has one: Arabic writes the meridiem after the digits, which on an RTL line puts it to their left; LRI lays the token out left-to-right and the meridiem lands on the wrong side (measured on the history rows: "8:00 م" where ar_SA writes "م 8:00"). FSI leaves it where the language puts it and still keeps the token whole. Outside RTL the two are indistinguishable: the first strong character is Latin ("PM"), FSI resolves to LTR, and the bytes an LTR locale renders do not move.

## SiteLinks.swift

- `SiteLinks` — links out of the app into if24-site, resolved to the language the interface is actually in.
- `SiteLinks.privacyPolicy` — the language comes from `Bundle.main.preferredLocalizations`, not from the device region or `Locale.current.language`: that is the localization the bundle actually resolved, so it also follows the per-app language override in Settings. A reader whose interface is German gets the German policy even on a device set to region US.
- `SiteLinks.sitePath` — English lives at the site root and maps to an empty segment. The bundle ships only the ten localizations listed, so the default arm is unreachable in practice, and it is the right answer anyway: a language without a page gets the English root rather than a 404.

## Strings.swift

- `strings` — type-safe access to the `Localizable.xcstrings` catalog: add an accessor here, then the matching key and translations in the catalog. Two key styles live here. Most strings are keyed by their English text, the catalog's source language. Strings whose English wording is expected to keep moving are keyed symbolically ("Editorial.fed", "About.medicalNote", "Footer.startEating") with the English in `defaultValue`. Not tidiness: rewriting a text key does not edit a string, it creates a new one, and the ten translations stay behind on the old key looking merely unfinished. Prefer a symbolic key for anything editorial or legal.
- `Locale.latinDigits` — the current locale with Western digits and the Gregorian calendar pinned. The app mixes two formatting paths: durations and dates go through `String(format:)` and `HistoryFormat`, which never localise digits ("118"), while an `Int` interpolated into `String(localized:)` is formatted by the locale, so in Arabic one screen would show "٦" beside "118". Pinning the numbering system puts both paths on one system. The language is untouched, so translations resolve normally and plural rules come from the real locale.
- `Date.FormatStyle.latinDigits` — use this rather than `Date.FormatStyle(locale: .latinDigits)`. `DateFormatter` takes its calendar from the locale it is given, so `f.locale = .latinDigits` is enough there; `Date.FormatStyle` does not — its calendar is a separate initialiser argument defaulting to `.autoupdatingCurrent`, so passing only `locale:` pins the digits and leaves the calendar on whatever the device uses. On an Arabic device that is the Islamic calendar, and the Sunnah settings would list "3 ربيع الآخر، 1448 هـ" where the fasts being scheduled are counted in Gregorian days.
- `strings.Editorial.goalReached(_:)` — the editorial once the goal is reached (overtime); `hours` is the plan goal in hours.
- `strings.Timer.closesAt(_:)` — "Closes 8:00 PM", the caption under the eating-window countdown.
- `strings.Timer.goalOvertime(_:_:)` — the overtime pill in the centre, e.g. "Goal 16:00 · +0:24". Goes through `String(format:)` rather than an interpolated `String(localized:)` so a translation can reorder the two arguments with `%1$@` / `%2$@`: Arabic needs the goal and the overage the other way round, and interpolation binds them by position. Same shape as `Editorial.beginsIn`.
- `strings.Timer.deepAutophagy` — the static phase chip in the overtime state.
- `strings.Footer.startEating` — the `.complete` primary and the three footer secondaries (`declineWindow`, `doneEating`, `notNowFast`) are each on their own named key. A footer secondary is defined by the primary beside it, and one generic verb cannot be right next to "Start eating", "Start fast" and "Continue fasting" at once. Named keys rather than English text (the exception `history.streak.unit` already makes): keyed by text, `Done` here would land on the plan sheet's button and take the paywall's with it.
- `strings.Footer.startEating` — opens the eating window. English drops the word "window", as the other nine locales do: the stat directly above the button says WINDOW OPENS, so the card names it once.
- `strings.Footer.declineWindow` — beside "Start eating" on `.complete`: declines the window. Not a postponement — `.complete` has no timeout, so the offer stands until the user acts, and this tap puts it out of reach for the rest of the cycle.
- `strings.Footer.doneEating` — under "Start fast" on `.eating`: ends the eating window early, behind the `EndEating` confirmation. Names the window rather than saying "Done", which reads as "close this screen" and gets tapped by accident. The key name is kept so the `eating.skip` flows and the catalog history stay put.
- `strings.Footer.notNowFast` — beside "Continue fasting" on `.eatingOver`: the window has already closed, so this defers the next fast rather than skipping anything. "Not now" is true here — a fast can be started from idle at any time.
- `strings.Footer.consequenceNearGoal(_:)` — the consequence slot of the `.complete` footer carries exactly one sentence, never none and never two, chosen by the path the fast ended on rather than by muting either: a fast ended short of its goal is the one case where the loss would otherwise be named nowhere. This is the missed-goal path: the day is not counted. Named through the streak, because that is what the person visibly loses and the term is fixed in all ten locales; it deliberately does not say the streak broke — whether it did is the streak's business. `%@` is the fast's length, the `%dh %02dm` token (ja renders 15時間50分).
- `strings.Footer.consequenceNearGoal(_:)` — the name is wrong on purpose: the line shows for every fast that fell short, by any margin, not only within the 15-minute near-goal threshold, and the honest name is `consequenceMissedGoal`. Renaming touches the key in ten locales for a string whose text does not change, and the catalog is edited by a build step that rewrites it unasked (`xcodebuild` reorders `Localizable.xcstrings`); a rename is free only while it is exact, so do it only in a release with time for a locale gate before submit.
- `strings.Footer.consequenceDefault` — the ordinary path: leaving the screen closes the record for good. Bound to the event, never to a position: "either action below" would be false in this layout (`Resume this fast` sits directly under the sentence and is the one action that does not end the fast), and spatial deixis survives neither RTL nor a reordering of the row. It holds only while exactly one exit from the screen leaves the fast alive; a second such exit re-arbitrates the sentence rather than translating around it.
- `strings.Complete.resumeFast` — the undo on the result screen. It promises the fast back, not the record gone — "Undo" would be ambiguous between the two — and the determiner is load-bearing: bare "Resume fast" reads as resuming fasting in general. Same family as `Reset this fast?` / `Delete this fast?`.
- `strings.EndFast` — the end-of-fast correction sheet. Its own namespace rather than more of `Sheet`: the two sheets ask about opposite ends of the same fast, and English carries the distinction in one adverb ("last") that other languages spell out on the verb, so a shared key would be true in English and undifferentiated everywhere else.
- `strings.EndFast.title` — the header of the picker states. Deliberately not `Sheet.whenDidYouEat` ("When did you last eat?"): at this moment the meal that ended the fast is the last one, so the old line is not false, it just stops distinguishing the two questions.
- `strings.EndFast.nearGoalTitle` — asks rather than states: at this distance from the goal the question is genuinely open.
- `strings.EndFast.minutesLeft(_:)` — whole minutes, never a duration format: Arabic agrees the numeral across six CLDR classes, four of them reachable under a 15-minute threshold, so "0 h 10" is not expressible there. The subject is the time, never the person: no encouragement in this line in any locale.
- `strings.EndFast.timerRanPast(_:)` — the overtime line. The subject is the app's own instrument, the timer, never the person's fast: the line shows precisely when the app assumes they have already eaten, so asserting their fast is running would assert something it does not know.
- `strings.EndFast.goalEndCaption(_:)` — "Yesterday · goal end", the caption under the exact-time value while it still sits where the sheet put it.
- `strings.EndFast.refusalTitle` — the refusal's own header: over a body that is no longer a picker, "When did you eat?" describes nothing on screen.
- `strings.EndFast.refusalReason(_:)` — names the fast in the way: a refusal that does not say what blocks it is a silent "no". Used when a different end time could still clear the clash, so offering a re-pick is honest.
- `strings.EndFast.refusalReasonUnavoidable(_:)` — used when the clash is on this fast's own start: no end clears it, so the copy points only at the one real way out instead of promising a re-pick.
- `strings.EndEating` — the confirmation in front of ending an open eating window early (`EndEatingConfirmSheet`), opened from `Footer.doneEating`.
- `strings.EndEating.keepOpen` — the safe action, the bottom one in the sheet.
- `strings.Sheet.backdateHint` — not drawn: the last-meal card would say in prose what the readout says in the value. Kept reaching the extractor on purpose, so the key holds its place in the catalog with its ten translations for the day the line comes back.
- `strings.Sheet.orSetExact` — the rule label above the exact-time control of the end-of-fast sheet. It lives in `Sheet` rather than `EndFast` because it is registered and translated under this name in all ten locales.
- `strings.PlanEditor` — the plan editor's own strings; it shares the rest of its copy with `Sheet`. This one button is separate because the paywall says the same English word for the opposite reason.
- `strings.PlanEditor.confirm` — applies the plan just chosen. It confirms a choice, so it takes the Done lexeme in every locale, never the OK one, which acknowledges rather than decides. The other half of the split `Done` is `Pro.restoredDone`.
- `strings.Meal.daysAgo(_:)` — "2 days ago", locale-aware plural.
- `strings.Meal.fastCountsFrom(_:_:)` — "Fast counts from 7:00 PM · 2h 40m in".
- `strings.Meal.exactTimeDone` — commits the wheel picker's value. It confirms a choice, so it takes the Done lexeme in every locale, never the OK one; split off the shared `Done` for the same reason as `PlanEditor.confirm`.
- `strings.Meal.scaleA11yValue(_:_:)` — "2h 40m ago, Yesterday at 8:00 PM", the VoiceOver value of the meal scale. The pause between the halves is punctuation, and punctuation is per-script: ja/zh speak `、`, not an ASCII comma. `ListFormatter` cannot supply it — even its narrow width injects a conjunction outside en (de "und", ar "و", zh "和", ko "및"), wrong for a two-part readout — so the pair is a localized template, like every joined string in this file.
- `strings.Meal.agoMinutes(_:)` — the readout is a whole template per shape (`agoMinutes`, `agoRelative`, `agoDays`), never a duration glued to an "ago": that word leads in de/es/fr/ar and trails in uk/pl/ja/ko.
- `strings.Meal.scaleTickHours(_:)` — the ribbon's hour tick label ("3h"), deliberately as short as the tick is wide; `scaleTickDays` is the day tick ("3d").
- `strings.Duration.daysShort(_:)` — compact whole days for summary aggregates ("23d"). Shares the short-unit key with the meal scale so all ten locales keep the same abbreviation.
- `strings.Duration.hoursSpelled(_:)` — "17 hours", the hour picker's rows spelled out. Plural categories live in the catalog, never as an `h == 1` here.
- `strings.Duration.planWindowSuffix(_:)` — "fast · 8h window", the trailing half of the plan sheet's value line; the leading number is drawn separately, in display type.
- `strings.History.lastStreakOverline` — the overline once the run is over: it renames the number as past and carries no verdict; the sentence under the number says what happened. Short class deliberately: "Streak ended" fills 100% of the slot in Ukrainian with no slack for a retranslation.
- `strings.History.emptyBackToFast` — the empty state's action while a fast is running. Names the destination rather than the direction: the nav chevron already means "back", and a second "Back" would read as two different ways out.
- `strings.History.firstNote` — the note under the counter on the first fasts. It states the rule rather than the outcome: a promise that the day was banked would be false after a fast that fell short of the goal, where nothing was banked. A streak day is a goal reached, and that is the one thing worth saying to someone who has not built one yet. No reproach: it never says what went wrong.
- `strings.History.export` — the export control is the system share glyph with no caption, so this is its VoiceOver label, and the word is `Export` without the `CSV` token: the format is named once, in the offer's benefit title.
- `strings.History.daysInARow(_:)` — the streak unit beside the big number. The number is drawn separately in display type, so the catalog's plural variants carry the word alone; CLDR categories live there, never as a `days == 1` here.
- `strings.History.streakEnded(_:lastGoalDate:)` — the sentence under the counter once the run has ended: what the number now is, the last goal day, and how the next one starts. The date is labelled as the last completed goal rather than the day the streak "ended": a streak becomes broken only after a later day is missed. No reproach and no sell — the way back is a goal reached, not a purchase.
- `strings.History.lastFast(_:)` — "Last fast · 16h 24m", the eating window's link into the history.
- `strings.History.groupMeta(_:_:)` — "9 fasts · 152h", the month group's aggregate.
- `strings.History.ofGoal(_:)` — "of 16h", the goal beside a fast that fell short of it.
- `strings.History.entryA11y(_:)` — VoiceOver label for the history entry point on the main screen.
- `strings.History.entryA11yLastStreak(_:)` — while the lost run is still on the pill, VoiceOver gets the word the pill cannot spend width on — "last", not "current" — so the number is not heard as a run still going.
- `strings.History.entryA11yNoStreak` — no streak to announce: the pill carries the history glyph and no number.
- `strings.Pro` — the paid tier: the offer screen, the Pro section of the About sheet, and the two one-time notices after the entitlement goes away. The product name is a literal everywhere and never templated: in Korean the particle that follows it is chosen by its pronunciation, which stops being decidable once the token becomes a `%@`.
- `strings.Pro.lockedDestinationHint` — the VoiceOver hint on any control the offer gates (the locked export in History, and the plan editor's confirm on the branch where the tap opens the offer instead of applying the plan). It says where the tap leads, not what it does, so each control's label stays truthful. A hint is never the only carrier: hints can be switched off, so every gated control also names the tier in a channel that cannot be suppressed. "the … screen" is load-bearing: after "opens", a bare product name lands in the "application" class in every language Apple ships, so a blind user hears "launches the separate IF24 Pro app", concludes it is not installed and does not tap. The non-breaking space inside the product name is the catalog's rule for it, in every occurrence.
- `strings.Pro.headline` — the framing line under the headline belongs to whichever benefit leads the list, so it changes with the entry point (`framingCustom`, `framingProtectedDay`, `framingExport`).
- `strings.Pro.framingExport` — the framing for the one door that names a benefit, the locked export row in History. It must say what the export benefit under it says without repeating its words: the line above a list is a frame, not its first item.
- `strings.Pro.wedge` — word for word as it stands in the store's promotional text.
- `strings.Pro.restoreShort` — two keys with one meaning: the short one is a link in the service block, paired with Privacy; `restoreFull` is a row and a button. They read alike in eight locales and differently in English and Arabic, which is what the split is for: merged, a locale would have to drop a grammatically required object in one of the two places.
- `strings.Pro.purchasedTitle` — S6 after money changed hands. Separate from `restoredTitle`: a purchase and a restore are not the same event to the person in front of the screen.
- `strings.Pro.approvedTitle` — S6 after an Ask to Buy was approved. The app does not name who approved it: several locales cannot do that without guessing a gender.
- `strings.Pro.purchasedBody(_:)` — the body of the paid S6, with the amount in it: a money moment names its sum. `%@` is `ProProductInfo.displayPrice`, the store's own string, already isolated and unbreakable (`paidPrice`). The sum deliberately does not end the clause: the Saudi riyal carries a full stop inside its currency form, and two in a row would land in the payment confirmation of the app's largest Arabic market.
- `strings.Pro.purchasedBodyNoPrice` — the same body when the store never answered with a price. Reachable only through an Ask to Buy approved in a session with no product: there is no Buy button without a sum, so `.purchased` cannot get here.
- `strings.Pro.paidPrice(_:)` — the amount as it goes into a sentence. Two things happen to it, both the app's job rather than the translator's: it is bidi-isolated, so currency and digits do not fly to opposite ends of an Arabic line, and its spaces become non-breaking, so "COP 29.900,00" cannot come apart across two lines at accessibility sizes. The glyphs are untouched: the sum reads character for character as in the price block.
- `strings.Pro.restoredDone` — the primary of the restored panel, the same full-width slot that carries Buy and Restore in the other states, on a screen that keeps its title, badge and check-marks. A result panel, not an alert, so the locale takes its Done lexeme, not its OK one. The other half of the split `Done` is `PlanEditor.confirm`.
- `strings.Pro.nothingToRestore` — the transient answer to a Restore that found nothing. Not a failure and not a state of its own: the store answered, the answer was empty. No full stop in any of the ten locales.
- `strings.Pro.statusInactive` — the About sheet's Pro row has four statuses (`statusInactive`, `statusActive`, `statusAwaiting`, `statusUnverified`), and their axis is the state of the entitlement, never the tier, the purchase or the price. `inactive` is empty in Japanese by decision, not by omission: the right-hand column of a settings row reads there as "the option you picked", and every attested candidate said something false.
- `strings.Pro.revokedTitle` — the two notices (edge 6 and edge 17 of the paywall spec) share one sheet at two moments: `revokedTitle` / `revokedBody` and `goalChangedTitle` / `goalChangedBody`.
- `strings.Pro.goalChangedTitle` — keyed by its own English text, deliberately: with a symbolic key and an empty default, the catalog compiler drops the entry, Foundation hands the key back verbatim, and the sheet renders `pro.notice.goalChanged.title` on screen. With the English source as the key that failure cannot be built.
- `strings.Pro.goalChangedBody(_:)` — `%@` is the spelled-out fallback goal ("16 hours"), the vocabulary the custom length was set in, so it compares against the 17 just lost without arithmetic.
- `strings.About.medicalNote` — closes the science section rather than sitting under a commercial row. On a symbolic key, deliberately: this is the legally heaviest line in the catalog and the most often rewritten, and every rewrite of a text key silently detaches the nine translations hanging off it. The advice to see a clinician stands before the examples, and the list of who is at risk is open ("especially if…"), not closed — a closed list reads as "everyone else is fine".
